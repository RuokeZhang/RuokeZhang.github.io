failure

During a big promotion our model’s TPM (500,000 tokens/min) was exhausted and many highlight requests started failing. My goal was to stabilize the service quickly without just dropping requests. I added a worker-side token bucket (Redis) so a task only calls the model if tokens are available, otherwise it gets delayed and retried later. I also tied autoscaling to token usage and queue length so we automatically add capacity when pressure rises. Within minutes the failure rate fell below 1% and the task queue stopped growing.

interpersonal conflict

On one project my teammate thought the prompt-engineering tasks were too simple and didn’t want to join. He asked me to do everything, but it was too much for one person because we needed to evaluate multiple model APIs on time. I set up a one-on-one chat to hear his concerns.

I said I understood the tasks felt basic, but I pointed out that delivering on time matters for the team’s reputation. I suggested we split the work: I’d take the repetitive parts while he focused on module design and strategic decisions. I also explained that finishing this well would lead to more challenging opportunities. He agreed, we delivered on schedule, and our work quality led to tougher assignments and a better working relationship.

new task

I was asked to build a quota management API and integrate it with DMP and real-time PV tracking. I wasn’t familiar with the ad business—especially A/B traffic setup and DMP—so I invested time understanding those first. I confirmed acceptance criteria with the PM, then split the work into core components: the quota query API, the allocation algorithm using urgency-based greedy approach, real-time PV tracking integration, and VID polling for A/B experiment traffic ratios.

For each component, I read docs, studied existing code, and talked to service owners. I also built a quick prototype to validate assumptions and added metrics and end-to-end tests. We delivered on time; the system raised campaign CTR by 3%, supports 5M daily users, and the prototype became the foundation for automation.

Template

During my internship at TikTok, another team in our department was short on engineers. They needed a new feature delivered in just one week to sync the Indonesian seller FAQs into our Seller Center chatbot’s RAG system.

I volunteered to own the task. Because the deadline was tight, I first aligned on scope: I committed to supporting a one-time import of the existing FAQ documents, and clearly said that recurring weekly updates would be a stretch goal, not a guarantee.

I spent half a day using internal docs and AI to understand the RAG pipeline, then wrote a short tech plan and got quick review from my mentor and the other team. After that, I used three days for development and two days for testing and fixing issues.

We shipped the feature on time, and on the last day I also managed to finish the recurring update flow, so operations could keep FAQs up to date without extra engineering work.

Are Right, A Lot

https://cdn.cms.amazon.jobs/4d/61/87e560ee49e2a823d213d2fccdc9/are-right-a-lot-transcript.pdf

  • 亚马逊 “多做正确的事”（Are right, a lot）领导力原则的核心是强判断力与良好直觉，而非固执己见，其本质是判断力的体现；
  • 该原则的重要性在于，亚马逊业务多处于高度模糊、需持续创新或优化的领域，关键决策的正确性远重于日常琐事的对错，需从复杂信息中筛选出对客户真正重要的核心信号；
  • 常见误解是将其等同于 “自己的想法必须占上风”，实则核心目标是为客户找到最佳答案（无关想法归属）；
  • 正确实践方式：召集合适的人参与反馈、倾听多元视角，领导者常最后发言，主动质疑自身固有信念，不执着于个人观点，全力追求对客户和业务最有利的结果。

死守标准招人 和 负责任地育人

  • 招人：哪怕项目再急、时间再紧，也不能降低标准去凑合招人。如果你招的人不能让团队变得更强，就不要招。 -育人：对你职业生涯帮助最大的经理，往往不是平时对你最客气的，而是那些能指出你的不足、逼你发挥潜力的经理。

Insist on the Highest Standards

不要怕标准太高显得不讲理，只有通过以身作则把标准传导下去，并彻底修复每一个问题，才能在激烈的竞争中留住客户。

Think Big

不要只盯着修修补补当下的业务，要敢于构想那些看似不可能、极具挑战性的未来，并抢在别人之前把它做出来。

Bias for Action

在商业中速度至关重要，且大部分决策不需要深思熟虑。

  • 双向门（Two-way doors）： 大多数决策都是可逆的。就像走进一个房间发现错了，退出来就行，没损失。这类决策必须快，在团队层面解决，不要过度分析。

  • 单向门（One-way doors）： 少数决策是不可逆的（或者很难回头）。这类决策才需要深思熟虑。

  • “崇尚行动”不代表可以牺牲“最高标准”（发布垃圾产品）。领导者的工作就是在“快”和“好”之间找到平衡点

  • 窗口期在关闭，几天能做完的事，不要拖几周。要把自己看作是在和时间赛跑，每天都要问：这一周结束时，我让客户变得更好了吗？

Frugality

  • 当你钱不够、人不够的时候，你才会被迫动脑子去想聪明的办法（Resourcefulness）。
  • 哪怕公司再有钱，也要像创业公司那样思考：拒绝铺张浪费，拒绝盲目扩招，用有限的资源逼出最大的创新，并把省下的成本用来取悦客户。

Project

Highlight

Introduction

In a nutshell, this project is for TikTok’s e-commerce team in Indonesia, where they sell products via livestreams. The idea is simple: after a livestream ends, our system automatically finds the best product moments, clips them into short highlight videos, and saves them for future use. Essentially, we detect interesting peaks during the stream and then use a multi-round LLM process to refine those clips. The result is a set of ready-to-use highlight videos that help boost engagement and sales.

LLM conversation

We run our LLM highlight system through several rounds of processing. In the first round, we take the full transcript and let the model pick out candidate excerpts that match certain product moments. In the second round, we refine those excerpts to focus on a single main product and remove anything off-topic. Then we go into a third round where two models, like ChatGPT and Gemini, critique the segments to ensure they’re clear and accurate.

In the fourth round, we do a final refinement where we only delete parts that were marked earlier, keeping the original wording intact. And finally, in the fifth round, we evaluate the final clips against a set of quality criteria—like making sure the product is clearly shown and the clip is coherent—before we finalize everything.
