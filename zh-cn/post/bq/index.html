failure

During a big promotion our model’s TPM (500,000 tokens/min) was exhausted and many highlight requests started failing. My goal was to stabilize the service quickly without just dropping requests. I added a worker-side token bucket (Redis) so a task only calls the model if tokens are available, otherwise it gets delayed and retried later. I also tied autoscaling to token usage and queue length so we automatically add capacity when pressure rises. Within minutes the failure rate fell below 1% and the task queue stopped growing.

Challenge

The most challenging project I worked on was the livestream highlight auto-clipping system at TikTok. It was challenging because it involved multi-stage LLM processing in a real production setting.

The hardest part for me was handling model rate limits under peak traffic. Each candidate segment could trigger multiple LLM calls, so token usage scaled quickly. During major promotional events, when the number of concurrent livestreams increased a lot, we started seeing rate-limit pressure, including throttled requests and accumulating retries.

To make the system stable, I implemented a global token-bucket mechanism before each LLM call, so we could control token consumption at the system level. If there were not enough tokens, the request would automatically retry with exponential backoff and jitter, which helped smooth traffic spikes instead of creating retry storms.

What made this particularly challenging was balancing throughput and stability. I needed to make sure the system stayed within provider limits without slowing the whole pipeline too much.

背景

我在TikTok做直播高光剪辑的时候，当时多轮LLM对话的效果一般，我们试图去improve 我想的方式是加入一个模型，就是在某一轮让两个大模型同时打分、评价，把他们的反馈再扔到下一轮里面 同事质疑我说这会增加费用，希望只优化当前prmonpt来提升模型标注效果。 后来我们拿100个视频片段测试，其中大约80个视频的标注效果得到了提升 于是团队同意采用我的方案 documentation

During my internship at TikTok, I worked on a livestream highlight generation system that used multi-round LLM conversations. At one point we found that the annotation quality was not very stable, so we discussed ways to improve it.

I proposed adding an additional step where two different LLMs would score and critique the generated clips, and their feedback would be fed into the next round of refinement. One of my teammates was concerned that introducing another model would increase cost, and suggested we only optimize the existing prompts.

Instead of debating the idea, I suggested we run a small experiment. We sampled around 100 video clips and compared the original pipeline with the new approach. In about 80% of the cases, the highlight annotations improved.

Based on these results, the team agreed to adopt the approach to improve the pipeline quality.

【背景】我是TikTok的一个实习生。 当时其它团队找到我 需要上线一个新的小feature，只有5天时间。 我需要用5天时间去熟悉代码，测试，并且上线。 这个小feature是把tiktok shop center的一些文档上传到他们的chatbot RAG知识库里面 【我是怎么缩小决策空间的？】我没有选择花时间去测试parse文档，而是要求运营在一天时间之内，把这些内容整理成Q A对，更新到spreadsheet中。因为这样做embedding和retrival是最直观、准确的。文档parsing的效果不确定，需要花时间修改chunking策略 【采取了什么行动】和负责该项目的成员，还有负责更新Q A的运营拉了一个会，运营整理QA的时候，我熟悉代码。开发完成后，交给运营人员在chatbot中用Q提问，准确率100%。【结果】用了四天时间就上线了，然后接着work on下一步：定期自动更新Q A到知识库里面 【反思】在开始执行前，先确定好“哪些是必保的”，就先完成那一部分。有多的时间再去优化

这个 feature 为什么当时重要：tiktok刚和tokopedia合并，商家的问题很多 你为什么能判断 QA 比 parsing 更适合 MVP：上网查阅了RAG的资料 你怎么验证“准确率 100%”：就是交给运营，把每个question都放到chatbot里面问了一遍，回答的和answer一致 后续自动更新具体是什么方向：就是定期RPC调用，embedding一次，然后注入elasticSearch

One example was during my internship at TikTok.

At that time, TikTok had just merged with Tokopedia, so merchants had many new questions. Another team asked me to help launch a small feature in only five days. The feature was to put TikTok Shop Seller Center content into our chatbot’s RAG knowledge base, so sellers could ask questions and get answers from the chatbot.

The main challenge was time. In those five days, I had to learn the existing code, implement the feature, test it, and launch it. Because the deadline was very tight, I knew I could not spend too much time trying every possible solution.

At first, one option was to directly parse the original documents, chunk them, create embeddings, and then connect them to the retrieval pipeline. But I thought that would be risky for an MVP. The parsing quality was uncertain, and if the chunking was not good, the retrieval result might also be poor. That would take more time to debug and tune.

So I first thought about what was most important for this launch. For me, there were three things: the answers had to be accurate, the solution had to be easy to validate, and we had to go live on time. I also read some RAG materials online, and based on that, I felt structured QA pairs would be a more reliable choice for a first version.

So instead of spending time testing document parsing, I suggested that the operations team spend one day organizing the key content into question and answer pairs in a spreadsheet. I made this decision because QA pairs were much more direct for embedding and retrieval, and they were also much easier to verify.

After that, I set up a meeting with the project team and the operations team to align on the format, the timeline, and who would do what. While they were preparing the QA pairs, I used that time to learn the codebase and implement the ingestion logic.

After development, we validated the result in a very simple way. The operations team entered each question into the chatbot one by one, and we checked whether the returned answer matched the expected answer. For the initial launch scope, the answers were all correct.

In the end, we launched the feature in four days, which was one day earlier than the deadline. After that, I continued working on the next step, which was to make the update process more automatic through a periodic RPC call, generate embeddings, and then inject the data into Elasticsearch.

What I learned from this experience is that when time is very limited, I should first decide what is absolutely necessary, deliver that part first, and then optimize later.

At Shanghai AI Lab, a new intern joined our team when we were working on dataset optimization with a pretty tight deadline. He needed to ramp up quickly on Isaac Sim and the basics of robotic training, otherwise it could slow down the project.

I decided to help because I had gone through the same ramp-up process myself, so I knew the learning curve was pretty steep. Also, our full-time team members were busy, so it wasn’t efficient for them to answer the same onboarding questions repeatedly.

【背景】在做多模态research的时候，我构建dataset的pipeline，我的目的是从360度全景视频中找出audio or visual event，并让Gemini对它们的方向进行标注。当时带我的phd正忙于毕业考试，所以我需要自己确认一个方案，怎么尽量找到全景视频的所有events.

【方案】我有两个方案 一个是把画面旋转四次 每个视角90度 然后让Gemini标注。另一个方案是，第一步旋转四次让Gemini标出primary view，然后第二轮标注是针对这个primary view ,左右各旋转30度和60度，一共得到五个视角给gemini标注，然后为标注出的角度算平均值，但是token消耗会更多。 我先定义了评估标准：direction accuracy和token cost。 然后我在一小批样本上比较两种方案。 结果发现方案二虽然 token 成本更高，但明显减少了漏标，而且方向标注更稳定。 基于这个结果，我决定先推进方案二，再把实验结果同步给 mentor。最后他认可了这个方案，我们用它继续做后续的数据构建。 【学习】我学到了在缺乏信息的时候，可以自己尝试不同方案的优缺点，并将结果呈现给mentor

During my multimodal research internship, I was building a dataset pipeline to detect audio and visual events from 360-degree videos and use Gemini to annotate their directions. At that time, the PhD student mentoring me was busy preparing for his graduation defense, so I couldn’t get timely guidance and had to make the decision myself.

The key decision was how to cover as many events as possible without making the annotation cost too high. I considered two approaches. The first was to rotate the panoramic video into four 90-degree views and ask Gemini to annotate each view. The second was a two-stage approach: first use four rotated views to identify the primary view, then generate additional nearby views around that primary direction and ask Gemini to annotate those as well, then average the predicted angles. This second approach used more tokens, but I expected it to improve direction accuracy and reduce missed events.

Since I didn’t want to decide based on intuition alone, I ran a small-scale comparison and evaluated both approaches on event coverage, annotation quality, and token cost. The second approach was clearly more accurate and captured events more reliably, while the token increase was still manageable. Based on that result, I decided to move forward with the second approach and then shared the evidence with my mentor, who agreed with the direction.

This experience taught me that when I can’t get immediate guidance, I should define the evaluation criteria first, test the main options quickly, and make a decision based on evidence rather than waiting for perfect information.

complex system dive deep

  • 这个系统为什么复杂
  • 当时遇到的具体问题是什么
  • 是怎么往下挖的

为tiktok商城的广告平台修改quota allocation api. 原来的广告曝光分配只支持选择人群描述，现在需要选择更细粒度的人群包(DMP)，还要加上real-time pv tracking。 首先，广告设置的时候是与A/B实验平台绑定的，我先要了解A/B testing platform的API， test setting的构成。因为每个广告会有不同的setting，每个setting又有自己的流量分配比例(20%)和freq_ratio 其次，广告需要考虑freq ratio. 所以一则广告的freq ratio是通过每个setting的freq ratio做weighted sum得到的。比如一则广告理论的impression floor是A，那么实际上我们只需要给它分配freq_ratio*A

One Dive Deep example was when I worked on the quota allocation API in TikTok Shop’s ad platform. Originally, the system only supported coarse audience types like new users or old users, but the new requirement was to support DMP audience packages.

What made the system complex was that quota calculation was coupled with the A/B testing framework. For a single ad, there could be multiple experiment settings, and each setting came with its own traffic allocation and frequency control rules. So before changing the API, I first dug into the experiment platform API and the structure of each test setting, to understand how traffic was actually divided.

Then I focused on freq_ratio, because the theoretical impression floor was not the same as the quota we should actually reserve. Each setting had its own freq_ratio, so the ad-level value had to be calculated as a weighted sum based on traffic split. For example, if the theoretical floor was A and the aggregated freq_ratio was 0.7, then the real quota should be treated as 0.7A.

What I realized was that this logic could not be applied only at the final allocation step. It had to stay consistent through the whole quota flow, including request building, validation, and the returned left quota. Otherwise, the system could say there was enough quota during validation, but behave differently during actual allocation.

So the real challenge was not just extending an API. It was making sure DMP audience, A/B experiment settings, freq_ratio, and real-time PV all followed one consistent quota framework. As a result, I was able to support the new requirement without creating inconsistencies across different parts of the allocation flow, which made the rollout much safer.

During my internship at TikTok, there was a department meeting where another team mentioned they needed to integrate the Indonesian seller FAQ into the RAG-based chatbot, but they were short on engineers. My team was focused on a critical deadline, so they couldn’t support the work.

After the meeting, I approached my mentor and volunteered to own this task. He connected me with that team, and I began driving the integration. I read their design docs, learned how the RAG pipeline worked, and worked closely with the operations team to make sure I understood their goals.

After about one week, the Indonesian seller FAQ was fully integrated into the chatbot. Internal tests showed about 95% answer accuracy, and the service went online ahead of schedule with weekly content updates.

Bias for Action

在商业中速度至关重要，且大部分决策不需要深思熟虑。

  • 双向门（Two-way doors）： 大多数决策都是可逆的。就像走进一个房间发现错了，退出来就行，没损失。这类决策必须快，在团队层面解决，不要过度分析。

  • 单向门（One-way doors）： 少数决策是不可逆的（或者很难回头）。这类决策才需要深思熟虑。

  • “崇尚行动”不代表可以牺牲“最高标准”（发布垃圾产品）。领导者的工作就是在“快”和“好”之间找到平衡点

  • 窗口期在关闭，几天能做完的事，不要拖几周。要把自己看作是在和时间赛跑，每天都要问：这一周结束时，我让客户变得更好了吗？

Frugality

  • 当你钱不够、人不够的时候，你才会被迫动脑子去想聪明的办法（Resourcefulness）。
  • 哪怕公司再有钱，也要像创业公司那样思考：拒绝铺张浪费，拒绝盲目扩招，用有限的资源逼出最大的创新，并把省下的成本用来取悦客户。

Project

Highlight

Introduction

In a nutshell, this project is for TikTok’s e-commerce team in Indonesia, where they sell products via livestreams. The idea is simple: after a livestream ends, our system automatically finds the best product moments, clips them into short highlight videos, and saves them for future use. Essentially, we detect interesting peaks during the stream and then use a multi-round LLM process to refine those clips. The result is a set of ready-to-use highlight videos that help boost engagement and sales.

LLM conversation

We run our LLM highlight system through several rounds of processing. In the first round, we take the full transcript and let the model pick out candidate excerpts that match certain product moments. In the second round, we refine those excerpts to focus on a single main product and remove anything off-topic. Then we go into a third round where two models, like ChatGPT and Gemini, critique the segments to ensure they’re clear and accurate.

In the fourth round, we do a final refinement where we only delete parts that were marked earlier, keeping the original wording intact. And finally, in the fifth round, we evaluate the final clips against a set of quality criteria—like making sure the product is clearly shown and the clip is coherent—before we finalize everything.
